Part One

Using Peepal

For students, teachers, staff, clinicians and institute administrators. No technical background needed.

1. What Peepal is

Peepal is a multi-tenant ERP that runs the daily operations of an institution: the people, the academic or clinical calendar, money, facilities, approvals and reporting, all in one place.

Three ideas explain almost everything about how the product behaves.

1.1 Every institution is a tenant

Your institution is a tenant with its own isolated data. You reach it either at a subdomain such as https://yourschool.roserp.workvar.com or through a path such as /yourschool/dashboard. Nobody outside your tenant can see your records, and every row of business data is filtered by tenant on the server.

1.2 The product adapts to your industry

Peepal supports several verticals. The industry chosen for your tenant decides which modules exist at all, and the vocabulary used across the interface.

IndustryWhat the sidebar emphasises
EducationStudents, courses, subjects, marks, exams, timetable, fees, hostel, transport, library
HealthcarePatients, appointments, OPD visits, admissions, laboratory, radiology, nursing, pharmacy, billing and claims
CorporateEmployees, payroll, leaves, approvals, finance, procurement, learning
NonprofitShared people, approvals, finance and reporting modules

Shared modules such as Employees, Leaves, Payroll, Approvals, Finance, Announcements and Reports appear for every industry. A hospital never sees Marks; a college never sees Radiology. This is enforced on the server, not just hidden in the menu.

Terminology layer. Words in the interface are configurable per tenant. A "Student" may read as "Trainee", a "Course" as "Programme", a "Department" as "Unit". If the labels in this guide differ slightly from your screen, the underlying feature is the same.

1.3 What you can see is decided in two layers

  1. Subscription decides which module families your institution has paid for.
  2. Access matrix decides, per role, which modules within that set you may view, create, edit or delete.

If a colleague sees a menu item you do not, the difference is almost always one of those two layers, not a bug.

2. Getting access and signing in

2.1 Accounts are created for you

Peepal has no public sign-up. Your institute administrator creates your account, either directly with a password, or by sending you an invitation email containing a one-time setup link where you choose your own password.

Invitation links expire. If your setup link no longer works, ask your administrator to resend the invite rather than trying to register yourself.

2.2 Email is not always your username

Some institutions run without staff or student email addresses. In that case you sign in with your Employee ID or Roll Number instead, and only administrators hold an email-based account. Your administrator will tell you which mode your tenant uses.

2.3 Signing in

  1. Open your tenant URL, for example https://yourschool.roserp.workvar.com/login.
  2. Enter your email (or ID) and password.
  3. You land on the Dashboard scoped to your role.

Your session is held in a secure, browser-managed cookie that lasts up to 24 hours. After that you are asked to sign in again. First-party cookies must be allowed for the Peepal domain, otherwise login will appear to succeed and then bounce you back.

2.4 First things to do

  • Change the password you were given, from My Profile.
  • Check that your name, department and contact details are correct, and report corrections to your administrator.
  • Open Notifications so you know where alerts arrive.

4. Roles and what each one sees

Peepal ships six built-in roles. Institutions layer custom roles on top of these, so your job title may be "Receptionist" or "Pharmacist" while the system still treats you as staff underneath.

RoleTypical scope
AdminFull control of the tenant. Creates people and accounts, configures roles, access, approvals, fees, payroll, academic years and holidays. Sees every report and the audit trail.
TeacherTeaching and clinical-practitioner work: attendance, marks, results, courses, subjects, exams, timetable, assignments, leaves, approvals assigned to them. In healthcare tenants this is the clinician role, with My Schedule.
StaffOperational work: employees, attendance, fee payments, facilities, inventory and procurement, billing and claims, front desk, leaves.
StudentSelf-service only: My Portal, My Fees, My Grades, My Hall Tickets, My Assignments, My Mess, plus attendance, marks, timetable, library and events in read-only form.
PatientHealthcare self-service: My Health, plus dashboard, notifications and profile. Nothing else.
Super adminPlatform operator, outside any single tenant. Manages tenants, plans, subscriptions, module configuration and platform analytics from the /super console.

4.1 Custom roles

Administrators create named roles under Organisation → Roles and grant them permissions per module under Access Control. Healthcare tenants are seeded with Receptionist, Doctor and Pharmacist roles automatically. A custom role can never exceed the ceiling of the base role it sits on, so a custom role built on staff cannot reach admin-only pages.

4.2 Role-locked pages

A handful of pages hold data that only exists for one role, and are therefore hidden from everyone else including administrators: My Portal, My Fees, My Grades, My Hall Tickets, My Assignments, My Mess, My Health and My Schedule. If you are an administrator wanting to see a student's fees, use Fees → Student Fees rather than the student portal.

5. Administrator: first-week checklist

Work through this in order. Each step depends on the ones above it.

Day 1 · Secure the tenant

  1. Receive your tenant URL and administrator credentials from the platform owner.
  2. Sign in and change the password immediately.
  3. Open Organisation → Org Profile and set the institution name, address, logo and identity policy (whether staff and students need email addresses).
  4. Confirm your subscription and enabled modules with support, so you are not configuring something you cannot use.

Day 2 · Structure

  1. Departments: create the organisational units everything else hangs off.
  2. Academic Years (education): define the current year and term.
  3. Holidays and Calendar: load the annual calendar so attendance and leave calculations are correct from the start.
  4. Roles and Access Control: create the roles your institution actually uses and grant least-privilege permissions.

Day 3 · People

  1. Employees: add staff and teachers. Creating an employee also creates their login account in the same step, so choose the role and either set a password or send an invite.
  2. Students: enrol students the same way, or use bulk upload for a whole intake.
  3. Admins: add any additional administrators, sparingly.

Day 4 · Academic or clinical setup

  1. Education: Courses, Subjects, Curriculum, Exams and exam types, Grading scheme, Timetable.
  2. Healthcare: Schedules for clinicians, Wards and Beds, Laboratory test catalogue, Pharmacy drug list.

Day 5 · Money and process

  1. Fees: Categories, then Structures per course, then Allocations, which generate each student's fee record and instalments.
  2. Payroll: Salary Templates, then Salary Assignments per employee.
  3. Leave Types and Approval Types / Approval Flows: define who approves what, in what order.
  4. Email: configure SMTP so invitations and notifications can actually be delivered.

Day 5 · Verify

  1. Sign in as one user per major role and confirm each sees only what they should.
  2. Submit one test leave request and approve it, to prove the approval chain works.
  3. Record one fee payment and download the receipt.
  4. Open Audit Trail and confirm your test actions were logged.
Order matters. Fees cannot be allocated before structures exist, structures need courses, courses need departments. Working top-down saves rework.

7. Everyday workflows

7.1 Bulk upload with CSV

Most list pages that accept large volumes carry a Bulk Upload button. The flow is the same everywhere.

  1. Click Bulk Upload and download the field guide, which lists every column, whether it is required, and the accepted values.
  2. Fill the CSV. Keep referenced values exactly as they appear in the application: department names, course codes, roll numbers and similar.
  3. Upload and review the validation result before committing.
  4. Rows referencing something that does not exist fail individually; the rest still import. Fix and re-upload only the failures.
Reference values are validated, not created. A misspelled department will fail the row rather than silently creating a new department. Create the reference data first.

7.2 Applying for and approving leave

  1. Open Leaves and submit a request against an available leave type.
  2. The request routes through the approval flow configured for that type.
  3. Approvers see it under My Approvals and in the notification bell.
  4. Each decision is recorded in the audit trail.

7.3 Recording a fee payment

  1. Go to Fees → Payments and search for the student.
  2. Select the outstanding instalment and record the amount, mode and reference.
  3. Download the receipt PDF, which is generated on the server.
  4. Confirm the balance updated in Fees → Overview.

7.4 Marking attendance

  1. Open Attendance, choose the date, course, semester and section.
  2. Mark the roster individually, or use bulk mark for a whole class.
  3. Use Attendance Summary for percentages and Shortage List to find students below the threshold.
  4. Attendance Export produces a downloadable file for external reporting.

8. Troubleshooting for users

SymptomMost likely cause and fix
Login succeeds then returns to the login pageCookies are blocked for the Peepal domain, or a captive-portal network is stripping them. Allow first-party cookies and retry outside the portal.
Signed out unexpectedlySessions last up to 24 hours. Sign in again; nothing is lost apart from an unsaved form.
A menu item a colleague has is missing for youYour role lacks the permission, or the page is role-locked. Ask your administrator to check Access Control.
A whole section is missing for everyoneThe module is not part of your subscription, or does not exist for your industry. Contact support.
Nobody can sign in at allThe subscription may have lapsed. Login is blocked without an active subscription. Contact your platform owner.
A banner says you are over quotaYour licensed student or employee count is exceeded. New records are blocked until an administrator upgrades the plan or removes records.
Bulk upload rejects rowsA referenced department, course or roll number does not match an existing record. Create the reference first, then re-upload only the failed rows.
Invitation email never arrivedCheck spam, then ask your administrator to confirm SMTP is configured under Organisation → Email and resend.
A page renders but tables are crampedDense admin tables and bulk tools are designed for desktop. Use a screen of at least 1280×720.
Reporting a problem. Include the time, your tenant name, your role, the page you were on, and what you expected to happen. That is usually enough for support to reproduce it without a follow-up round trip.

Escalation path: institute administrator, then Peepal support, then engineering on call.

9.4 Multi-tenancy and access, in three layers

  1. Industry. models/access_industries.go on the server and MODULE_INDUSTRIES in lib/access.ts decide whether a module exists for the tenant at all.
  2. Subscription. models/subscription_modules.go maps fine-grained modules onto coarse subscription modules; effective modules are enforced through myAccess and the access field middleware. Super admin is exempt; administrators are not.
  3. Access matrix. Per-tenant role to module CRUD grants (AccessRule), edited at /org/access-control, with a role ceiling derived from resolver requireRole guards so navigation never links to a forbidden page.
Golden rule. Never query a business table without a tenant filter.

  1. Install Node.js 18+ and pnpm.
  2. Install Go 1.21+.

12. Codebase conventions

12.1 GraphQL first

The backend exposes GraphQL and a shrinking set of REST routes side by side. Default all new work to GraphQL.

  • Add features as gqlgen resolvers in a per-module graph/<module>.resolvers.go.
  • Do not add REST routes or handlers unless GraphQL genuinely cannot serve the case: file streaming, third-party webhooks, CSV upload.
  • Existing REST endpoints (auth and login, terminology, super-admin, bulk upload) stay as they are.
  • On the frontend, call GraphQL queries and mutations rather than REST through axios.
  • When extending an existing feature, match the API it already uses. Default any net-new surface to GraphQL.

12.2 Small files, split logic

Keep route files thin and put the real work in components/pages/.... Split large modules into several files rather than growing one. The same applies on the backend: one resolvers file per module, one bulk schema file per resource.

12.3 Naming across the boundary

gqlgen emits camelCase. A few legacy TypeScript types are snake_case, and nothing transforms between them automatically. A mismatch produces silent undefined reads on read and an HTTP 422 on write. Map explicitly at the slice boundary with fromGql and toGqlInput helpers.

12.4 Authentication

  • The JWT lives in an httpOnly cookie named peepal_token, valid for 24 hours. Never store tokens in localStorage.
  • Redux holds only a hasSession marker. That marker can drift from the real cookie, so always await logout before navigating, and call useHydrateSession() on login pages.
  • Resolvers enforce authorisation with requireAuth and requireRole. Frontend guards are for user experience only.
  • COOKIE_DOMAIN must be empty on localhost. A domain value is rejected by the browser and freezes a stale auth cookie, which shows up as me() returning the previous user.

12.5 Data integrity

  • There are no database-level foreign keys. Bulk CSV rows validate their references through handlers/bulk/reference_lookups.go; a non-blank miss fails that row only.
  • Deleting a person cascades every relation through graph/cascade_delete.go and deleteUserCascade. Extend those helpers whenever you add a per-person table.
  • Audit logging is automatic for GraphQL mutations. REST handlers, including bulk row submission, must call the audit package's Record themselves.

12.6 Vocabulary

Shared UI must read its words from the terminology tokens and roleLabels.ts, never hardcoded education nouns. Role identifiers in the database never change; only the display label does.

13. Building a feature end to end

13.1 First day

  1. Read this guide, the root CLAUDE.md, and frontend/ONBOARDING.md.
  2. Install the toolchain and bring up both services.
  3. Sign in and browse the in-product technical docs at /{tenant}/docs/dev and /super/tech-docs.
  4. Open /super/db-visualizer to see the schema as an interactive map.
  5. Run a focused Playwright smoke test if credentials allow.

13.2 The workflow

  1. Model. Add a GORM model under backend/models with tenant_id and the UUID hook.
  2. Migrate. go run main.go --migrate, once.
  3. API. Write a gqlgen resolver in graph/<module>.resolvers.go. Guard it with requireAuth and, where relevant, requireRole.
  4. Access. Register the new module id so it appears in the access matrix, and add it to MODULE_INDUSTRIES if it is industry-specific, or ROLE_LOCKED_MODULES if it is single-role self-service.
  5. Frontend. Thin app/.../page.tsx, real logic in components/pages/....
  6. State. Apollo for server data, Redux for auth, chrome and terminology.
  7. Navigation. Add the entry to BASE_MODULES and navConfig.ts so the tile appears.
  8. Bulk. If the resource needs CSV import, add a schema_*.go under handlers/bulk/ and drop <BulkUploadButton resource="..." /> on the page. For resources whose creation has side effects, prefer a per-row GraphQL mutation loop instead.
  9. Tests. Extend the Playwright hierarchy carefully; it is ordered with LIFO cleanup.
Forgetting step 4 is the classic mistake. A module missing from ROLE_LOCKED_MODULES leaks into the admin sidebar (administrators hold a blanket view grant) and then 404s when opened.

13.3 Regenerating GraphQL

Regenerate the schema from live introspection rather than hand-editing generated artefacts. After a migration, regenerate the frontend schema documentation with pnpm gen:schema. Note that a small number of mutations have been hand-wired into the generated code, so run a full regeneration deliberately and review the diff.

14.4 Release checklist

  1. Announce the window to administrators if downtime or a migration is expected.
  2. Back up: take a snapshot, or confirm the latest automated backup is green.
  3. Deploy the backend first, kept compatible with the old frontend where possible.
  4. If models changed, run --migrate once against the production DSN from a controlled shell.
  5. Deploy the frontend and reload PM2.
  6. Smoke test: super-admin login, one tenant login, one GraphQL list query, one file upload, one PDF download.
  7. Monitor error logs and analytics for 30 to 60 minutes.
  8. Rollback by restoring the previous PM2 binary and build. Restore the database only if the migration was destructive; prefer forward fixes.

14.5 Schema discipline

  • Use expand, migrate, deploy, contract for breaking changes.
  • Never run destructive SQL in production without a backup and a written rollback plan.
  • Mount backend/uploads/ on durable storage. Photos and generated PDFs live there, outside the binary, and must be in the backup policy.
